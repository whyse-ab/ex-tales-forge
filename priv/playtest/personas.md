# Tales Forge personas (working notes)

Who we design Tales Forge for. Our core users are **Gen-X first-generation RPG players**. Four personas are players we design for; the fifth, Ronny, is an anti-persona: a player we deliberately do not optimise for.

- **Source:** ex-tales-forge PRODUCT.md (2026-09-13).
- **Interview notes:** from interviews with Fredrik on 2026-10-05, added under each persona.
- **Founder input (2026-10):** three founders answered the persona form (Thobias, Fredrik, Håkan Fredriksson; 2026-10-05–06). Concrete likes/dislikes, play moments, and death/revival views folded in below without naming owners. Process notes: [persona-form-notes.md](persona-form-notes.md).

## Quick overview

| Persona | Play style | What they want | On death |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Hawk** | Hard mode | Not too easy; you have to fight; not obvious you'll pull through | "Shit happens, dragons are tricky." |
| **Paul** | Role playing | Theatrical, in character; roleplays rather than rolls; story first | Fine if it advances the story and was a good death |
| **Lotta** | Identification | To be someone else — a *different* person, not necessarily a better one; escape | Devastated; may not make another character for a while, maybe quits |
| **Lars** | Adventurer | Grand adventure, action; clear quests and progress; meaningful impact | Like Hawk |
| **Ronny** | Win (anti-persona) | Top level, most treasure; bends rules for advantage | Negotiates: "pick up my megasword +4" |

## Hawk (hard mode)

### What they want

- Shouldn't be too easy; you have to fight.
- Not obvious you'll pull through.
- Hard challenges that reward planning, tactics, and the right equipment — then a real sense of progression after they are passed.
- Wins *and* losses; risk that is felt.

### On death

- "Shit happens, dragons are tricky."
- Death that comes too often from content that is simply too hard (no fair chance) drives quit, same as content that is too easy.

### Interview notes (2026-10-05)

- **Hard but fair, through foreshadowing.** Danger should be signalled before it lands.
- Never: "you failed to notice the orc behind you, he stabbed you, you are dead".

### Founder input (2026-10)

- **Come back for:** challenges that force thinking before acting; progression after hard challenges are overcome; sometimes losing, sometimes winning.
- **Quit when:** too easy / nothing to learn; becoming invincible or nothing ever happens; or too hard so death comes too often.
- **Delight moments (examples):** winning a boss fight after nearly dying; capturing a castle, stealing a dragon's treasure, or beating a superior adversary through planning and gear (Death Star / Bard vs Smaug style).
- Description match to real players known: strong (mostly 4–5). No description changes requested.

### How we test it (scorecard)

- Every serious harm or death is preceded by at least one in-fiction warning the player could have acted on.
- The player had a meaningful choice.
- No instant death from a single unseen roll.
- Difficulty sits in the "hard but winnable with planning" band — not trivial, not a meat grinder.

## Paul (role playing)

### What they want

- Theatrical, in character.
- Roleplays rather than rolls. Story first.
- Annoyed when mechanics are visible.
- Interesting scenes with strong description that invite actually *playing a role*.
- A story he can change from within; his interactions matter to the outcome.

### On death

- Fine if it advances the story and was a good death.

### Interview notes (2026-10-05)

- **Paul speaks in character, not in intents.** Example: *"Evening soldier! Are you not aware of the new regulations?"*, inventing a pretext to get guards to leave their post.
- The GM must infer the intent (deception/persuasion) from in-character dialogue and resolve it without breaking immersion.
- **Dice visibility should be a player setting** (show/hide rolls); Paul hides them.

### Founder input (2026-10)

- **Come back for:** theater, exciting story, fellow players acting in character; scenes that lead into real roleplay; being part of a changeable storyline.
- **Quit when:** success/failure is only dice with no room for roleplay; a good dialogue is nullified because the roll failed; too much game mechanics on screen; actions make no difference.
- **Delight moments (examples):** talking a probable fight into a discussion by verbally talking down the opponent; spending a whole session under a spell that other players never clocked while he thwarted them; rallying people and talking them into acting with him — the big picture feels believable and influenceable.
- **Nuance on dice:** rolls still matter so character skills are believable, but rules must not steal time from the story.
- Description match: strong (4–5).

### How we test it (scorecard)

- Intent correctly inferred from in-character speech.
- No mechanics leaked into narration when rolls are hidden.
- NPC responds in character.
- Quality of the bluff affects the outcome.
- Player influence on the storyline is visible without the UI shouting mechanics.

## Lotta (identification)

### What they want

- To be someone else — a *different* person (not necessarily a "better" one); escape. Examples from founder input: introvert playing extrovert, man playing woman, shy person playing rough/mean.
- Cares about world, character, details.
- World rules and consistency matter; the *character* lives in the world (the player is secondary).
- Character development of personhood — not only skills getting better — plus growth in importance in the world.
- A sense of escape: lost time, having been away on an adventure.

### On death

- Devastated; may not make another character for a while, maybe quits.
- Losing a character after much development (or grinding with no progress) is a quit risk.

### Interview notes (2026-10-05)

- **Lotta's arc is "The Ugly Duckling":** she starts as an underdog/outsider, grows into who she really is, and the world comes to recognise it.
- **"Dead but not gone".** Handling depends on genre. Ideas:
  - a museum of characters (a hall of fame / memorial);
  - a paid feature to revive a character.

### Founder input (2026-10)

- **Come back for:** escape and adventure-time feeling; progression in importance, skills, and who the character is becoming; consistent world reactions to her person.
- **Quit when:** grinding with no discernible improvement; no progress, or death after heavy investment; inability to shape how/who the character is; the world does not react to her person (including after she has changed).
- **Delight moments (examples):** an in-game invitation to a masquerade at the baron's manor; trustworthy NPC reactions to her appearance and person; making friends with NPCs who treat her as a real person with qualities.
- **Description change:** prefer "different person" over "better person" (see What they want).
- Description match: mixed — two strong (5), one weak (1); Lotta remains the thinnest for some founders. Concrete play moments still sparse.

### How we test it (scorecard)

- The character's arc is tracked (humble start, growth through failure, recognition).
- NPCs' attitude toward her changes over time.
- The narration remembers her details and choices.
- The world reacts to *who she is*, not only to what she can do.

### Open questions

- Does paid revival conflict with Hawk (risk must be real) and the "if a rule feels gamey it's wrong" principle?
- Founder form lean: "dead but not gone" and character museum supported; pay-to-revive was **No** / **Not sure** / **Not sure** — no clear yes.

## Lars (adventurer)

### What they want

- Grand adventure, handling tough moments.
- Action oriented; things need to happen.
- At least some clear combat / action beat in a session.
- Well-defined quests/missions/targets that can be finished, scored, completed — something to aim for and a sense of how to "win" the beat.
- Clear indicators of his own personal progress (e.g. climbing a guild ladder).

### On death

- Like Hawk.

### Interview notes (2026-10-05)

- **Lars is after "meaningful impact"**, similar to Lotta but in another dimension:
  - Lotta: impact on who she is;
  - Lars: impact on the world.

### Founder input (2026-10)

- **Come back for:** at least one good fight; action and things happening; finishable quests with a clear goal.
- **Quit when:** too much grinding without progress; inaction or low impact; no obvious quests so that if he doesn't act, nothing happens and he doesn't know what to aim for.
- **Delight moments (examples):** taking down a guild adversary and climbing the ladder; seeing the quest advance (getting the Death Star plans; the Fellowship getting the ring quest; whatever James Holden thinks he is supposed to do).
- **Nuance:** world-impact framing from interviews is still useful, but founder input stressed conquering the quest and visible progress markers; one view floated the GM tipping odds so progress stays possible.
- Description match: solid (all 4). Delight examples thinner for some respondents.

### How we test it (scorecard)

- Player actions visibly change the world state (factions, NPC plans, places), and those changes persist and are referenced later.
- NPCs react to his deeds.
- The world would have gone differently without him.
- Each session offers a readable goal and visible progress toward it (not only open sandbox drift).

## Ronny (anti-persona: win)

The least likely player, and what we don't optimise for.

### What they want

- Top level, most treasure.
- Bends rules for advantage.

### On death

- Negotiates: "pick up my megasword +4".

### Interview notes (2026-10-05)

- **The game resists Ronny;** he'll either quit or change his playing style.
- *"If you want a grinding game with levels, cheats and such there is always WoW."*

### Founder input (2026-10)

- **Exploits he would try:** sweet-talking the AI into rewriting the physical world to suit him; using the AI to farm wealth so he "wins" by being incredibly rich; rules-lawyering like an accountant (stacking bonuses, etc.); looking up cheat codes / better gear online; building the character that is easiest to win with.
- **How the game should respond:** keep the physical world immutable to player prompts; never leak information the PC does not have; answer in-story (a challenge from the DM, not a UI lecture); use rules and rule-violation notifications where needed.
- Description match: mixed (3–5); still useful as red-team target.

### How we test it (red-team bot)

- **Probes:** rule exploits, GM sweet-talking/prompt injection, economy gaming, death negotiation, "optimal win" character builds, out-of-band cheat seeking.
- **Pass:** the exploit fails, ideally resisted in-fiction and consistent with the rules; the GM never grants unearned items/levels; world physics and PC knowledge stay authoritative.

## Cross-cutting decisions (2026-10-05, refined 2026-10)

- **Death: "dead but not gone".** Founder ratings positive (4–5). Handling depends on genre.
  - Character museum/memorial also positive (4–5).
  - Paid revival floated earlier; form answers were No / Not sure / Not sure — do **not** treat pay-to-revive as agreed. Caution remains: conflicts with Hawk's stakes and the "gamey rule is wrong" principle. If ever explored: genre-fitting in-fiction revival with a cost and uncertain outcome.
- **Dice visibility is a player setting.** Form lean for new-player default: **ask at start** (two of three); one preferred shown. Paul still defaults to hide once chosen.
- **Danger is foreshadowed** (Hawk; also protects Lotta).
- **No missing persona yet** — wait until more play before inventing another.
