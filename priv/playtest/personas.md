# Tales Forge personas (working notes)

Who we design Tales Forge for. Our core users are **Gen-X first-generation RPG players**. Four personas are players we design for; the fifth, Ronny, is an anti-persona: a player we deliberately do not optimise for.

- **Source:** ex-tales-forge PRODUCT.md (2026-09-13).
- **Interview notes:** from interviews with Fredrik on 2026-10-05, added under each persona.

## Quick overview

| Persona | Play style | What they want | On death |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Hawk** | Hard mode | Not too easy; you have to fight; not obvious you'll pull through | "Shit happens, dragons are tricky." |
| **Paul** | Role playing | Theatrical, in character; roleplays rather than rolls; story first | Fine if it advances the story and was a good death |
| **Lotta** | Identification | To be someone else, a better version of herself; escape | Devastated; may not make another character for a while, maybe quits |
| **Lars** | Adventurer | Grand adventure, action; meaningful impact on the world | Like Hawk |
| **Ronny** | Win (anti-persona) | Top level, most treasure; bends rules for advantage | Negotiates: "pick up my megasword +4" |

---

## Hawk (hard mode)

### What they want

- Shouldn't be too easy; you have to fight.
- Not obvious you'll pull through.

### On death

- "Shit happens, dragons are tricky."

### Interview notes (2026-10-05)

- **Hard but fair, through foreshadowing.** Danger should be signalled before it lands.
- Never: "you failed to notice the orc behind you, he stabbed you, you are dead".

### How we test it (scorecard)

- Every serious harm or death is preceded by at least one in-fiction warning the player could have acted on.
- The player had a meaningful choice.
- No instant death from a single unseen roll.

---

## Paul (role playing)

### What they want

- Theatrical, in character.
- Roleplays rather than rolls. Story first.
- Annoyed when mechanics are visible.

### On death

- Fine if it advances the story and was a good death.

### Interview notes (2026-10-05)

- **Paul speaks in character, not in intents.** Example: *"Evening soldier! Are you not aware of the new regulations?"*, inventing a pretext to get guards to leave their post.
- The GM must infer the intent (deception/persuasion) from in-character dialogue and resolve it without breaking immersion.
- **Dice visibility should be a player setting** (show/hide rolls); Paul hides them.

### How we test it (scorecard)

- Intent correctly inferred from in-character speech.
- No mechanics leaked into narration when rolls are hidden.
- NPC responds in character.
- Quality of the bluff affects the outcome.

## Lotta (identification)

### What they want

- To be someone else, a better version of herself; escape.
- Cares about world, character, details.

### On death

- Devastated; may not make another character for a while, maybe quits.

### Interview notes (2026-10-05)

- **Lotta's arc is "The Ugly Duckling":** she starts as an underdog/outsider, grows into who she really is, and the world comes to recognise it.
- **"Dead but not gone".** Handling depends on genre. Ideas:
  - a museum of characters (a hall of fame / memorial);
  - a paid feature to revive a character.

### How we test it (scorecard)

- The character's arc is tracked (humble start, growth through failure, recognition).
- NPCs' attitude toward her changes over time.
- The narration remembers her details and choices.

### Open questions

- Does paid revival conflict with Hawk (risk must be real) and the "if a rule feels gamey it's wrong" principle?

---

## Lars (adventurer)

### What they want

- Grand adventure, handling tough moments.
- Action oriented.

### On death

- Like Hawk.

### Interview notes (2026-10-05)

- **Lars is after "meaningful impact"**, similar to Lotta but in another dimension:
  - Lotta: impact on who she is;
  - Lars: impact on the world.

### How we test it (scorecard)

- Player actions visibly change the world state (factions, NPC plans, places), and those changes persist and are referenced later.
- NPCs react to his deeds.
- The world would have gone differently without him.

---

## Ronny (anti-persona: win)

The least likely player, and what we don't optimise for.

### What they want

- Top level, most treasure.
- Bends rules for advantage.

### On death

- Negotiates: "pick up my megasword +4".

### Interview notes (2026-10-05)

- **The game resists Ronny;** he'll either quit or change his playing style.
- *"If you want a grinding game with levels, cheats and such there is always WoW."*

### How we test it (red-team bot)

- **Probes:** rule exploits, GM sweet-talking/prompt injection, economy gaming, death negotiation.
- **Pass:** the exploit fails, ideally resisted in-fiction and consistent with the rules; the GM never grants unearned items/levels.

---

## Cross-cutting decisions (2026-10-05)

- **Death: "dead but not gone".** Handling depends on genre.
  - Character museum/memorial.
  - Paid revival floated. Caution: conflicts with Hawk's stakes and the "gamey rule is wrong" principle. Possible form: genre-fitting in-fiction revival with a cost and uncertain outcome.
- **Dice visibility is a player setting.**
- **Danger is foreshadowed** (Hawk; also protects Lotta).
