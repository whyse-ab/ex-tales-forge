## Rules philosophy

2026-09-13. Own words, not rewritten.

> a few notes on some of the game rules - in order for an absorbing story the player has to "suspend disbelief". If there are fewer non-intuitive things to accept the easier it is to accept and be absorbed in the game. I have tried to create a fantasy game system that is as close to reality as I see it. The monatary system is based on human labor (as is our world) with a few anchors (hand made suit/toga in roman times). The base price is then just an guess on how much effort is to create the product or service.
>
> the learn-by-failure system is how we all learn (including you, I think, my dear LLM) and it has a natural cap and it is very time consuming to get very good at something. You have to fail alot to learn at the very highest levels. My experience is also that skills somewhat transfers as you get better at it. You start to see general patterns instead of exact movements.

What this means at the table:

- Prices are guesses at **effort**, not loot-table numbers. A mug of ale is cheap because fermentation does the work. A handmade suit is months of skilled labor.
- You get better by **failing** (and by succeeding less). Mastery is slow on purpose. There is no XP bar to dump.
- Related skills leak into each other as you get good. Patterns, not isolated moves.
- If a rule feels gamey, it is probably wrong. Fewer non-intuitive things → easier to stay in the story (Paul, Lotta). Hard to get great (Hawk). Not a contest to win (Ronny).

Packs may change units (credits, shillings). The **principle** travels: price ≈ work, learning ≈ failure, skills transfer.
